WHEAT CROP – COMPLETE AGRICULTURE INFORMATION

1. INTRODUCTION

Wheat is one of the most important cereal crops in India. It is mainly grown during the Rabi season, which generally starts after the monsoon season.

Wheat is widely cultivated in Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan and other states.

Wheat grains are used to prepare chapati, roti, bread, biscuits, flour and other food products.

2. WHEAT GROWING PERIOD

Wheat is generally sown between October and November in Punjab.

The first fortnight of November is considered an important sowing period for many recommended varieties in Punjab.

The crop generally takes around 140–160 days to mature, depending on the variety, sowing time, soil and weather conditions.

Some varieties mature earlier, while late-sown varieties may have a shorter growing period.

Wheat harvesting in Punjab generally takes place around March and April.

3. WATER REQUIREMENTS

Wheat requires sufficient water for healthy growth and grain development.

Under irrigated conditions, many wheat crops require approximately 4–6 irrigations. The exact requirement depends on soil type, rainfall, temperature and sowing conditions.

Important irrigation stages include:

- Crown Root Initiation (CRI): Around 20–25 days after sowing.
- Tillering: Development of additional shoots.
- Jointing: Development and elongation of the stem.
- Flowering: Development of flowers and grain formation.
- Milking: Grain begins filling with a milky substance.
- Dough stage: Grain becomes harder.

The CRI stage is particularly important for root development.

Excessive irrigation can cause waterlogging and damage crop growth.

Farmers should adjust irrigation according to soil moisture and weather conditions.

4. MAJOR WHEAT DISEASES

A. Yellow Rust (Stripe Rust)

Yellow rust is an important wheat disease in northern India.

Symptoms:
- Yellow or orange powder-like spots appear on leaves.
- Spots generally form long stripes.
- Disease can spread under favourable cool and humid conditions.
- Severe infection can reduce grain production.

Prevention:
- Grow disease-resistant varieties recommended for the local area.
- Regularly inspect wheat leaves.
- Consult an agricultural expert when symptoms appear.

B. Brown Rust (Leaf Rust)

Symptoms:
- Small round orange-brown spots appear on leaves.
- Spots may spread across the leaf surface.
- Severe infection can reduce photosynthesis and crop yield.

Prevention:
- Use locally recommended resistant varieties.
- Monitor the crop regularly.
- Seek expert advice if infection is observed.

C. Loose Smut

Loose smut is a seed-borne fungal disease.

Symptoms:
- Wheat ears may become black and powdery.
- Infected grains are replaced by black fungal spores.
- Infected ears may appear earlier than healthy ears.

Prevention:
- Use certified disease-free seeds.
- Follow recommended seed treatment practices.
- Use varieties suitable for the local region.

D. Karnal Bunt

Karnal bunt is a fungal disease affecting wheat grains.

Symptoms:
- Some grains become partially infected.
- Infected grains may contain black powder.
- The affected grains can develop an unpleasant smell.

Prevention:
- Use recommended resistant varieties.
- Use certified seeds.
- Follow agricultural university recommendations.

5. WHEAT FERTILIZER REQUIREMENTS

Wheat requires important nutrients for healthy growth.

Nitrogen:
- Supports leaf growth and plant development.
- Helps in tillering and green leaf development.

Phosphorus:
- Supports root development.
- Helps in early plant growth.

Potassium:
- Supports plant strength.
- Helps plants tolerate environmental stress.

Fertilizer requirements depend on soil fertility, variety and cultivation method.

Farmers should preferably use soil testing and follow recommendations from their local agricultural university.

6. SOIL AND WEATHER REQUIREMENTS

Wheat grows well in fertile and well-drained soil.

Suitable conditions include:
- Fertile loamy soil.
- Proper drainage.
- Cool weather during early growth.
- Suitable temperature during grain development.

Waterlogging can negatively affect wheat growth.

High temperatures during grain filling can cause early maturity and reduce grain weight.

7. BENEFITS AND USES OF WHEAT

Wheat is an important source of carbohydrates and provides energy.

It also contains:
- Protein
- Dietary fibre
- Vitamins from the B group
- Minerals such as iron and zinc

Common uses:
- Chapati and roti.
- Bread.
- Biscuits.
- Pasta.
- Wheat flour.
- Other food products.

Whole wheat products generally contain more dietary fibre than refined wheat flour.

8. WHEAT HARVESTING

Wheat is harvested when the crop reaches maturity.

Common signs of maturity:
- Plants turn golden yellow.
- Grains become hard.
- Stems and leaves become dry.

In Punjab, harvesting generally takes place during March and April, depending on the variety and sowing date.

After harvesting, wheat grains are separated through threshing and dried to a suitable moisture level before storage.

9. IMPORTANT FARMER ADVICE

- Always use certified seeds.
- Select varieties recommended for the local area.
- Avoid excessive irrigation.
- Inspect crops regularly for diseases.
- Use fertilizers according to soil testing.
- Do not apply pesticides without identifying the actual problem.
- Consult the nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or agricultural university for disease diagnosis and treatment.
